I run Gatepath Realtors, a land-sales company based in Kenya. We sell
subdivided plots — mostly around Ruiru/Kiambu — to two kinds of buyers:
people living in Kenya who visit our sites in person, and Kenyans in the
diaspora (UK, US, Gulf states) who buy remotely and trust us to represent
their interests honestly. Our public-facing motto is "Your Interest Is Our
Priority," and we mean it literally — our whole pitch against competitors
is that we're more transparent and better organized than the informal land
brokers people are scared of.

I need you to design the internal console my staff and I use to actually
run the business day to day. I'm going to describe how the business
actually works and what each part of the tool needs to let us do — not how
to draw it. I want you to bring your own design instincts and make
something that feels premium, confident, and a little unexpected — not a
generic admin-panel template. Surprise me.

WHO WE ARE

modern, never cartoonish or filled.

===============================================================================
THE STORY OF A CLIENT, START TO FINISH — read this before designing anything
===============================================================================
Someone hears about us — Instagram, a friend, a radio ad, our CEO
personally, a referral partner — and fills in an inquiry form on our
website naming a project and a plot they're interested in. That inquiry
lands with my staff, who need to work it like a sales lead: contact the
person, qualify them, maybe book them a site visit so they can walk the
actual land (or a virtual tour if they're abroad). If they're serious, they
pay a deposit.

The moment that deposit lands, something specific happens in Kenyan land
sales that I need the tool to respect exactly: the client is issued an
"Offer Letter" — a real signed document, but a lighter one than a full sale
agreement. It's provisional. They then usually keep paying in installments
over months. Only once they've paid the FULL price does the real, binding
"Agreement" become valid — and only then can I, as CEO, sign it. Before
that point, the Agreement literally should not be presentable as a real
document. This two-stage gate is core to how we protect ourselves and our
clients legally, and I need the internal tool to make that gate obvious and
impossible for my staff to bypass by accident.

Once a client is fully paid up and signed, we still owe them a title deed —
and before we ever hand over land, my team manually checks the government
land registry to confirm the title is genuinely clean. That's a real,
manual, accountable step someone on my team does and logs.

Meanwhile I'm running a small sales team — a few agents/CROs who each own
a slice of these leads — and I need to see who's actually converting
inquiries into paying clients versus who's coasting.

===============================================================================
WHAT EACH AREA OF THE TOOL NEEDS TO DO FOR US
===============================================================================

HOME — When I or a manager opens this tool first thing in the morning, I
want the state of the business in one glance: how much money actually came
in this month, how many people are physically coming to see plots today,
how much unsold inventory we're sitting on across every project, and —
importantly — whose installment payments are falling behind, because
that's money we're owed and someone needs to chase it. This is a "what
needs my attention right now" screen, not a report.

AGENT PERFORMANCE — I want to hold my sales team accountable. I need to see
each agent ranked by how well they're actually converting the leads
assigned to them into closed, paid deals — not just activity, results. If
someone's leads are going stale without follow-up, I want that visible
without having to dig for it.

LEADS — Every inquiry that comes in needs to visibly move through stages
as my team works it: brand new, being actively worked, won (they're now a
paying client), or lost. My staff work leads by physically moving them
through these stages as things happen — it should feel tactile and
immediate, not like filling out a form. If someone already has a site
visit booked, that needs to be obvious on their card so nobody double-
books or forgets they've already shown real intent.

CONTACTS — This is our single source of truth on every person who has
ever engaged with us. For each one I want to know instantly: how many
plots have they bought from us, and how much money have they put in with
us total, across everything. My team also needs quick one-tap ways to call
or email someone from here. Separately — and this matters — I want a clear
warning list of any plot we've sold or booked where nobody has actually
logged a title verification check yet. That's a compliance gap I never
want hiding silently.

DEALS — This is my proof-of-sale record — the list I'd show at a board
meeting or to an auditor. It should only ever contain sales that are
completely done: the client paid in full AND I personally signed the
Agreement. Nothing provisional belongs here. I want it to feel final,
clean, and authoritative — the opposite of a messy spreadsheet.

INSTALLMENT TRACKER — Most of our clients pay over time, not in one lump
sum. For each of them I need to see clearly what they've actually paid
against what they owe, and be able to nudge someone who's behind with one
click. Company-wide, I want one honest number: of everything we're owed
across every payment plan running right now, what percentage has actually
landed in our account. That number is one of the most important in the
whole business to me.

INQUIRIES QUEUE — This is where my staff formally review a serious buyer
before anything legal happens — their personal details, and critically
their next-of-kin information, which Kenyan land transactions require. I
need to be able to approve or reject an inquiry with full context in front
of me. And — this is the single most important workflow in the whole
system — once someone's approved, I need to see their Offer Letter and
their Agreement as two clearly separate things, each only becoming real
and signable at the right moment (deposit paid vs. fully paid), never
confusable with each other, and I need to be able to sign each one myself
digitally the instant it's ready.

SITE VISITS — My team needs to see, at a glance, which days have visits
booked, who's confirmed versus still pending, and be able to reschedule or
mark a visit complete without friction. If a booking has just sat there
unconfirmed past its own date, I want that flagged — that's a client we're
about to lose through sheer neglect.

PLOT INVENTORY — This is our actual warehouse: every project we're
selling, every individual plot within it, its size and price, and whether
it's available, booked, or sold. My team needs to browse the whole
catalog easily, and drill into any single plot to see who's interested in
it and — critically — whether we've actually verified its title is clean
yet, with a record of who checked and what they found. Selling a plot
without that check logged should feel visibly wrong on this screen.

CAMPAIGNS & CONTENT — Our public website needs to stay fresh, and I don't
want to need a developer every time. My non-technical staff should be able
to swap the photos and videos for a specific project, upload a new
brochure, write and publish blog posts, and see which referral partners
are actually bringing us business.

STAFF ACCOUNTS — I control who has access to this whole system and at what
level — myself as the only one who can sign legal documents, managers, and
field agents below them. I need to invite new team members, see everyone
at a glance, and be able to change someone's access level or cut them off
entirely if I need to.

SYSTEM SETTINGS — The quiet, necessary stuff: my team editing their own
profile, choosing what they get notified about, and being able to pull a
data export when someone needs a report. Doesn't need to be exciting.

Don't ask me how to lay any of this out, what shape the cards should be,
or how many columns a table needs — that's your job, and I want to see
what a genuinely great designer does with this brief, not what I'd have
